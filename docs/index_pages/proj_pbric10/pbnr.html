Park Bike & Run

Last summer 2025 I met an extraordinary human being, we went on a very long walk, she encouraged me to start running more again (I had apprehension post-injury to do so) and she listened to me talk and realize in real-time how much I enjoy it.

At some point I described parkrun. and floated an idea of integrating it into some kind of endurance project. One of us ended up suggesting what I have formalized as PB&R. The rules:

1. Cycle between parks which host* a regular parkrun

2. Cover a minimum of 5km following the parkrun course**

3. Timing starts at the beginning of the first bike stage and stops when you reach your bike after the last run stage

* or have ever hosted
** as closely as possible

For all FAQs I would say use common sense e.g. the fastest way of doing a PB&R is to start the run from where you parked the bike and end it in the same place even if this makes the run >5km.

The inaugural version follows a route I’m calling Inner Circle 10 (see maps), and it is a PB&R of the ultra variety as the run distance is >42km. As long as the challenge follows the rules above, it counts. For un/self-/supported styles rules see the section on FKT.com.

Gear & Logistics

The project is conceptually simple but logistically quite complicated. These are the things that must be planned beforehand and how I chose to tackle them:

-1 Routes for both cycling and running, and how to navigate them

Plotted a route passing through 10 parks (for 50km run) forming a circular around central London. First I selected the parks, then I used Strava to consolidate both cycle and run stages into a single .gpx file. I loaded the route on the watch and started navigation so that it would appear in the live link map along with my position. I exported the .gpx and loaded it as a map layer in Google Maps, this allowed me to have the route on my phone which I anchored to the bike handlebar. This doesn’t give you Google navigation prompts but it does show the route and it moves the screen conveniently as you move. The run stages I looked at the map on my watch. Consider deactivating turn-alerts. 

-2 Parking logistics, locking bike and bag

If you do PB&R you will be parking your bike a fair amount (it’s in the name). My strategy was an attached U-Lock, a small (12L) backpack, and a helmet with a vent hole big enough to thread the U-Lock. I used a small padlock on the backpack and I locked everything together, frame, backpack, helmet. Most parks have bike racks. In Battersea I used a temporary fence protecting some machinery. I wore a mesh belt throughout and put keys and phone there when I ran.

-3 Nutrition and water availability

When plotting the route I marked one water tap per run stage. These were critical on the 21st of June as it was hot. I carried 1 * 750ml (bike bottle) 1 * 500ml and 1 * 650ml vessels, also one plastic drinking cup for convenience. I only ended up using the bigger bottles because I was stopping at water taps so much. Nutrition part is boring; I had gels, energy drink from powder (mulberry flavour, not bad), and dates. Gels and drink had electrolytes.

-4 Transition stretching

Planned but not implemented the following: standing cat cow stretches before the bike, high knees before run, and calf stretches while on the bike. Not doing these did not seem to have a huge effect.

-5 Gear meaning clothes

A Burberry trenchcoat and a colourful Prada shirt and corduroy trousers. But really, I opted for Tri-shorts, basically, half tights with minimal padding for cycling … I also wore a vest/sleeveless shirt for the first time ever and it was a great call as I perspire heavily and this garment type really maximizes airflow. Also important, a hat.

-6 Gear meaning cycle repair stuff

In a saddle bag I carried a minimal toolset for adjusting bits (allen keys) and changing a tube. If you don’t know how to do this, get someone to teach you.

-7 Gear meaning anything else

A few extras I considered necessary; small portable battery, spare top, small towel (this was really useful), blister plasters (did not use).

This is what I did about the logistics, I’d be happy to share the doc I used to organize myself as well as the .gpx file. Each project attempt will be different depending on length, time of year, your paranoia wrt circumstances …

Interlude 2

One of the attractive features of ultra-endurance is the flow that is achieved by focusing on the same effort for long periods of time. This can be a meditative experience associated with both positive and negative qualia. PB&R forces more directed attention at all stages and it interrupts any meditative flow with urban cycling.

In lieu of deep insights I had an idea for a film. It is a time-travel film where the main character travels to the future to find himself, there, he discovers that his future self has traveled to the past to find himself. Semi-stranded in the wrong time they have one more chance to fid themselves or return home. They have to choose what to do with their ast time-jump. They can return home, or they can try to predict where (or when haha) they would go to find themselves. The climax is the final time-jump and it resolves the movie as both of them chose to travel back in time to one of their first memories.

Details and People

At 4:14 when I started, there was a very low cloud on the marsh-grass. Birds were loud.

Southwark park was closed so I had to run around it.

Rotherhithe tunnel is not so bad (at about 5 am).

Lots of people like ending their Saturday night sitting on a bench on their phone in Burgess Park.

Battersea Power Station looks cool and I still haven’t been.

The sun is a deadly laser.

Dogs don’t know how to queue for water.

I am grateful to friends/coworkers who accompanied me at any point.

DF appeared at the end of Wormwood Scrubs run and we cycled to Hampstead Heath. This was critical because had I been alone I would have hammered the uphills. ZH met me at Finsbury Park before the run and left afterwards towards Hackney Marshes. D&G met me at the lowest and most interesting point of the project, right after the Highbury Fields run. I saw ZH and R&J at the beginning of Hackney Marshes run. I saw KC towards the end of the Hackney Marshes run, as she was cycling she accompanied me for a bit. More friends joined afterwards and we hung out around the Olympic Park.

I had a really rough time in Highbury Fields. It is an extremely repetitive course, a tilted oval so half is up and half is down-hill. It was probably 30C around 13:30 or so and it was incredibly busy. In ultra-verbiage the death march is the pace you carry when your brain wants to walk.

Finally, I reached my bike and was going through heat-exhaustion assessment and for the first time hitting some positive (bad) answers. I was feeling light-headed, a little disoriented, but no more than that, just mildly spaced-out. Then, with a slightly concerned look, D&G appeared behind me. It took me a second to register them. It didn’t look like it, but I was overjoyed (only slight hyperbole). We talked about the project, their day-plans, and they accompanied me to the water tap which once again I was sharing with small canines. I left Highbury Fields feeling much better than when the run ended.
